Your tap water may be harming your cat and plants — and you don’t even notice

By Plantas & Gatos — practical content for cat guardians and plant lovers

Many people trust tap water for everything: drinking, cooking, watering plants, and even filling the pet’s bowl. After all, if it’s potable, it should be safe, right? Not always. What seems harmless may hide an invisible problem that directly affects those who depend on that water: your cats and your plants.

The invisible problem in tap water

The treated water that reaches your home goes through rigorous purification processes to ensure our safety. This eliminates harmful microorganisms but also adds chemicals that, while safe for us, may be harmful to other living beings when consumed daily.

What’s really in your water

According to Brazilian drinking water standards, tap water contains substances such as:

Chlorine (between 0.5 to 2 mg per liter) → Legally required as a disinfectant, but it can irritate cats’ digestive systems and “burn” the tips of plant leaves.

Fluoride (0.6 to 0.9 mg per liter) → Added to prevent cavities, but cats are more sensitive to this compound than humans.

Metals like lead and copper → Harmless in trace amounts for us, but they can overload the kidneys of small animals over time.

Other treatment chemicals → Byproducts of the process that accumulate in the body with continuous use.

To put this into perspective: a 5 kg cat receives a concentration per body weight about 14 times greater than a 70 kg adult drinking the same water.

Warning signs in your cat

Cats already have naturally sensitive kidneys, and tap water may be making things worse without you realizing it. Watch out for:

  • Refusal to drink water or searching for other sources (dripping taps, vases)
  • Frequent urinary problems
  • Dull coat and tired appearance
  • Less energy to play
  • Preference for running water instead of a still bowl

Studies show that about 30% of elderly cats develop kidney problems, and the quality of water consumed throughout life may accelerate this process.

Warning signs in your plants

If you water your plants with tap water and they aren’t thriving, there may be a connection. Chlorine and other chemicals cause:

  • Leaf tips that are dry or burned
  • Yellowing without apparent reason
  • Slow or stunted growth
  • White crust on the pot (accumulation of mineral salts)
  • Altered soil pH (most plants prefer slightly acidic soil)

Why does this happen?

The difference lies in size and sensitivity. Cats process proportionally much more liquid through their kidneys than humans, and plants have delicate systems that react poorly to excess chemicals. What is “little” for an adult may be “a lot” for smaller, more sensitive beings.

Practical (and simple) solutions

The good news is that you don’t need big changes or high costs:

For your cat:

  1. Let the water sit → 24h in an open container eliminates most chlorine
  2. Use a simple filter → Basic carbon models already make a big difference
  3. Occasional mineral water → Especially if your cat already has a history of kidney problems
  4. Water fountains → Many cats prefer them and drink more

For your plants:

  1. Water rested for 48h → Removes chlorine and balances pH
  2. Collect rainwater → Naturally acidic and additive-free (just store it properly)
  3. Filter before watering → The same filter you use for your cat works for plants

How to know it’s working:

  • Notice if your cat drinks more water after the change
  • Track if urinary problems decrease
  • See if plants become greener and grow more vigorously
  • Check if the soil stops forming that white crust

ANd then...

What seems like just “tap water” may be silently impacting your cat’s health and your plants’ development. With official data showing that our water contains levels safe for humans — but not necessarily ideal for other living beings — small adjustments make all the difference.

The solution is neither complicated nor expensive. With simple changes in how you provide water, you can prevent health problems in pets and see your plants thrive.
