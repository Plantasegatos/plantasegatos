Stubbs: The Cat Who Was Mayor for 20 Years

Imagine a small community surrounded by the spectacular landscapes of Alaska. Quiet streets, snow-covered mountains, curious tourists, and a way of life that seems to follow its own rhythm.

Now imagine that the most famous political figure in this place wore no tie, gave no speeches, attended no meetings, and probably considered a good nap more important than any administrative commitment.

His name was Stubbs. He had four paws, whiskers, a remarkably short tail, and an extraordinary ability to win over voters who, technically speaking, had never voted for him.

Welcome to Talkeetna, where politics acquired a rather unusual representative.

A Small Community and a Remarkable Character

Talkeetna is located in the American state of Alaska, near Denali National Park. With its historic architecture, impressive scenery, and welcoming atmosphere, it has become a familiar stop for visitors exploring the region.

But among majestic mountains and outdoor adventures, an unlikely attraction began competing for tourists' attention: an orange cat named Stubbs.

He was not particularly interested in politics. In fact, there is no record of him ever expressing an opinion about public budgets, transportation, or urban development.

His greatest talent was something else entirely: being a cat.

The Birth of a Feline Authority

The story began in 1997, when Stubbs was still a kitten. He was taken in by Lauri Stec, who was associated with Nagley's General Store, a traditional business in Talkeetna.

His name came from his physical appearance: an extremely short tail. The English word stub can refer to something short or truncated.

Stubbs grew up surrounded by counters, merchandise, residents, and visitors. The store became his territory, while the humans naturally assumed supporting roles in its administration.

Eventually, the cat acquired a title that no other resident could carry with quite the same elegance: honorary mayor of Talkeetna.

But Did He Really Win an Election?

This is where the story becomes particularly interesting.

For years, a popular account claimed that residents, dissatisfied with the human candidates in a 1997 election, had chosen Stubbs instead.

It is an irresistible story. After all, who has never imagined replacing an entire political campaign with a cat sleeping on a desk?

There is just one problem: that official election never happened.

Talkeetna is an unincorporated community and has no formal municipal office of mayor. Stubbs therefore never won an official election or acquired governmental authority.

His title was symbolic. The election story became a popular legend, amplified by news reports and international curiosity.

Yet that hardly diminishes his achievement. Without a political party, a campaign platform, or promises, Stubbs gained something many politicians spend their careers seeking: public recognition and devoted admirers.

A Royal Routine Without Office Hours

While human officials deal with meetings, paperwork, and endless appointments, Stubbs developed a rather unconventional administrative style.

His headquarters was Nagley's General Store, where he welcomed visitors, watched the activity around him, and rested whenever he felt like it.

Tourists visited the store to meet him, take photographs, and, when the mayor was in the mood, offer a few affectionate strokes.

There were no speeches, ribbon-cutting ceremonies, or press conferences. At most, visitors could expect a sleepy glance, a walk across the counter, or a magnificent display of feline indifference toward human affairs.

And there was one particularly amusing detail.

Stubbs became famous for drinking water mixed with catnip from a margarita glass. Yes, a cocktail glass for a feline mayor.

Of course, it was not an alcoholic margarita. The glass was simply part of the unusual ritual that contributed to his fame.

An important reminder: this story is not a recommendation to offer human beverages to cats. Alcohol is toxic to felines, and any plant-based preparation must be assessed for safety.

The Day the Mayor Faced a Crisis

Not every chapter of Stubbs' life involved naps and admiration.

In 2013, he was seriously injured in a dog attack. His injuries included a punctured lung and a fractured sternum, requiring intensive veterinary treatment.

The incident drew widespread attention and expressions of support from admirers.

Stubbs survived and eventually returned to his routine after recovering.

We do not know whether the experience changed his views on public safety. But given the feline temperament, it is easy to imagine that he developed an even greater appreciation for high, secure, comfortable places.

An International Celebrity

Over time, Stubbs became far more than a familiar face in Talkeetna.

News reports, photographs, and stories about the four-legged mayor traveled around the world. Visitors arrived in the small community hoping to meet this unusual character in person.

The cat became an affectionate symbol of the area and one of its best-known attractions.

His role, however, remained entirely honorary. Actual regional administration continued to be handled by the appropriate human authorities.

Stubbs seemed perfectly content with a responsibility better suited to his talents: receiving attention, inspiring affection, and reminding visitors that life need not always be taken so seriously.

Twenty Years of an Unlikely Story

Stubbs died on July 21, 2017, at the age of 20, a remarkably advanced age for a domestic cat.

His family announced his passing and remembered his personality and the affection he had received throughout his life.

For two decades, this little orange cat had watched a local curiosity become an internationally recognized story.

He never signed laws, managed public funds, or won an election.

But he left something no official document could capture: the memory of an animal who brought identity, humor, and affection to a community.

The Real Lesson of Mayor Stubbs

Perhaps the most beautiful part of this story is also the simplest.

Stubbs did not need to understand the significance of his title. He did not need to know the tourists who traveled to meet him or understand why his photograph appeared in newspapers around the world.

He simply lived his life as a cat.

And by doing so, he brought people together, inspired smiles, and helped create a shared sense of identity.

The election was a legend. The office was honorary. But the affection was real.

Stubbs may never have been a real mayor. But he was undoubtedly a very dearly loved cat.

And here is a question: if a cat ran for mayor of your town, would you vote for him?
