The Power of Talking to Plants: A Conversation That Helps Everyone

Have you ever caught yourself talking to your plants? If so, you’re in great company — and science actually has some interesting things to say about it.

Do Plants Really “Hear”?

Even though plants don’t have ears, fascinating research shows that they’re sensitive to sound vibrations. A study from the University of Western Australia found that plant roots can “sense” sounds and grow toward specific frequencies, such as the sound of running water.

South Korean biologist Mi-Jeong Jeong conducted experiments indicating that certain sound frequencies can stimulate the opening of stomata (the “pores” on leaves) and boost the expression of growth-related genes. So technically, your kind words produce vibrations that your plants can, in some way, perceive.

The Green Placebo Effect

But here’s the most interesting twist: the biggest benefit of talking to plants may be for you, not for them. When you talk to your plants, a few things happen:

You pay more attention to them. While chatting with your fern, you naturally observe it more closely — noticing yellowing leaves, dry soil, or pests. The result? You take better care of it.

You create a mindfulness ritual. Those few minutes spent talking to your garden are a pause in the chaos of daily life. It’s meditation disguised as gardening.

You reduce stress. Studies show that interacting with plants lowers cortisol (the stress hormone) and improves mood. Therapeutic horticulture is a recognized practice in psychology.

The CO₂ Factor

There’s also the carbon dioxide theory: when we talk, we exhale CO₂ — exactly what plants use for photosynthesis. Speaking up close to your monstera might give it a tiny extra boost of carbon. A microscopic help, yes — but still technically real!

So…

Your plants probably don’t understand when you tell them about your day — but that doesn’t matter. The act of talking to them creates connection and a sense of commitment. And if that makes you feel better and helps your plants grow healthier, everyone wins.

So go ahead and keep talking to your little green friends without guilt. In the end, gardening has always been about cultivating life — including our own inner peace.
