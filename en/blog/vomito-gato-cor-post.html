Cat Vomit: What the Color Is Trying to Tell You

You just heard that unmistakable sound, rushed to the living room, and… there it is. Your cat has vomited again. The first instinct is to grab a towel (or paper, depending on your level of disgust), but before you clean it up at lightning speed, take a quick look. It might seem gross, but the color of that vomit could actually be telling you something important about what’s going on with your feline friend.

When Vomiting Is “Normal” (Yes, It Happens)

Cats vomit — it’s part of feline life. They’re meticulous self-groomers and often swallow hair while cleaning themselves, sometimes forming hairballs they need to expel. An occasional vomit, especially if your cat ate too quickly or is eliminating hair, isn’t necessarily a reason to panic.

But frequency and appearance matter — a lot.

Decoding the Colors

White or clear foam: usually means your cat’s stomach was empty when they vomited. It’s pure stomach acid, which can happen when they go too long without food or have mild indigestion. If it’s a one-time thing, it’s probably nothing serious — but if it becomes routine, check with your vet. It could indicate gastritis or other digestive issues.

Yellow: indicates bile, the digestive fluid produced by the liver. It often appears when a cat vomits on an empty stomach, during fasting, or when there’s mild liver or intestinal irritation. Again, if it’s occasional, don’t stress too much — but frequent yellow vomit deserves a vet visit.

Brown or black: is a red flag. It may contain digested blood, suggesting something more serious like ulcers or internal bleeding in the digestive tract. Don’t wait — take your cat to the vet right away.

Green: isn’t a good sign either. It can mean excessive bile or, worse, that your cat ate a toxic plant. If you keep plants at home (and who doesn’t?), check whether any could be dangerous. Lilies, pothos, and dieffenbachia (also known as dumb cane) are notorious feline hazards.

Red or pink: is pretty straightforward — there’s fresh blood present. This could come from mouth irritation, ulcers, or digestive tract wounds. Bright red, in particular, signals active bleeding and is a veterinary emergency that requires immediate attention.

When to Worry

A single vomit? It happens. But some situations call for urgent care. If your cat vomits several times a day, seems lethargic, has lost their appetite, or you notice blood in the vomit, don’t wait it out — see a vet.

Also, take note if there’s a lot of hair in the vomit. It could mean an intestinal blockage — long-haired cats are especially at risk. If vomiting is accompanied by constipation or signs of discomfort, seek professional help.

The Role of Plants in All This

Since we’re talking about green vomit and toxic plants, it’s worth emphasizing: many decorative plants can be dangerous for cats. They’re naturally curious and love nibbling on leaves — sometimes out of boredom, sometimes because they think it’s salad. The problem is that plants like lilies, pothos, and dieffenbachia can cause anything from mild irritation to severe poisoning.

If you saw your cat chewing on a plant and then vomiting, mention it to your vet. The more details you share, the easier it is to pinpoint the cause.

Observe, Care, and Act

The rule is simple: know your cat and watch for changes. An occasional vomit may not mean much, but strange colors, unusual patterns, or behavioral changes deserve attention. Your cat relies on you to notice when something’s off — and believe it or not, a carefully observed vomit could save their life.

So next time you hear that unmistakable sound, take a breath, have a quick look before cleaning, and trust your instincts as a cat parent. When in doubt, always consult your vet. It’s better to overreact than to miss something serious.
