Feline Grimace Scale: The 2026 Update Every Cat Owner Needs to Know

What is the Feline Grimace Scale?

The Feline Grimace Scale (FGS) is a scientifically validated tool for assessing acute pain in cats based on changes in facial expressions. Developed in 2019 by the research group of Dr. Paulo Steagall at the Université de Montréal, the scale evaluates five facial action units (AUs) that change when a cat is in pain.

Each unit is scored from 0 to 2, with a maximum total score of 10. Scores equal to or greater than 4 indicate the need for analgesic intervention.

The five action units of the FGS

The scale evaluates the following facial expressions, listed in order of clinical reliability:

  • Eye position (orbital tightening): ICC 0.84 — Excellent reliability
  • Ear position: ICC 0.81 — Good reliability
  • Head position: ICC 0.71 — Moderate reliability
  • Muzzle tension: ICC 0.47 — Poor reliability
  • Whiskers position: ICC 0.34 — Poor reliability

These inter-rater reliability indices (ICC) were confirmed by the study of Steagall et al. published in The Veterinary Journal in December 2025, which analyzed the dimensional structure of the scale and the importance of each AU. The conclusion was straightforward: trust the eyes and ears. Muzzle tension and whisker position are the least reliable units.

Why do cats hide pain?

Understanding the need for the FGS requires understanding an ancestral feline behavior. The cat occupies a unique biological position: it is a small predator, but also prey for larger carnivores. Over millennia, evolution selected individuals capable of concealing signs of vulnerability — displaying pain or weakness attracts predators and signals vulnerability to territorial competitors.

Unlike social animals such as dogs and humans, who use vocalization to seek help from the group, cats evolved to deal with their problems in a solitary and silent way. This preservation of the appearance of "normality" is such an effective self-defense tool that, by the time a cat finally shows obvious signs of pain, the pathological process is usually already at an advanced stage.

A cat's silence is not the absence of pain. It is pure survival instinct.

The January 2026 update: brachycephalic cats

In January 2026, the Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery formally published the study by Marangoni et al., which investigated the applicability of the FGS in brachycephalic cats — flat-faced breeds such as Persian, Exotic Shorthair, and Scottish Fold.

The study included 28 brachycephalic cats undergoing ocular surgery. Researchers collected 95 video images and compared photo-based assessments with real-time evaluations conducted by four trained raters.

The results confirmed that the FGS is highly reliable and responsive for detecting pain variations even in cats with atypical facial morphology. However, one important finding emerged: the flat anatomy of the Persian and Exotic distorts the classic facial landmarks of the scale — especially muzzle tension and whisker position — which can lead to overestimation of pain when assessment is based solely on static photographs.

The recommendation is clear: for brachycephalic cats, always prioritize real-time assessment over static image analysis.

A photo can lie

This is one of the most practical findings of the 2026 update. A photograph freezes a single moment — the cat may be blinking, yawning, or tensing for a reason completely unrelated to pain. In brachycephalic breeds, this distortion is even more critical: the naturally "closed" facial morphology mimics pain signals even when the animal is in perfect wellbeing.

Real-time assessment considers movement, posture, behavioral context, and facial expression dynamically. If an immediate veterinary visit is not possible, send a video — never an isolated photograph.

How any cat owner can use the FGS

The Feline Grimace Scale is available for free as an app for iOS and Android, developed by the Steagall Laboratory at the Université de Montréal. The app works offline, is available in Portuguese, English, Spanish, and Chinese, and guides users through each step of the assessment with reference photos for each AU.

How to use it:

  • Observe the cat at rest, without interacting, for at least 30 seconds
  • Evaluate each of the five expressions from 0 to 2, prioritizing eyes and ears
  • Add up the scores (maximum: 10)
  • A score equal to or greater than 4 indicates the need for veterinary evaluation

The app is available at felinegrimacescale.com.
