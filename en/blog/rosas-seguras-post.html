Safe Roses: The Perfect Valentine's Gift For Cat Owners

Few flowers carry as much symbolic weight as the rose. For thousands of years it has stood for love, power and desire, and it remains the most traditional gift for Valentine's Day. Yet anyone sharing a home with a cat often hesitates before accepting a bouquet: is it actually safe?

The answer, thankfully, is reassuring. The rose is one of the safest flowers that exist for cats. When there is a problem, it usually isn't the rose itself, but the flowers that tend to ride along in the same vase.

A flower with history

The bond between the rose and love is nothing new. In Greek mythology, the red rose is tied to the story of Aphrodite and Adonis: according to legend, the flower sprang from the blood Adonis shed, becoming from then on a symbol of love that endures even loss.

Centuries later, the rose found itself back at the center of the political stage in medieval England. The Wars of the Roses, the conflict that split the English throne in the fifteenth century, took their name precisely from the emblems of the two rival houses: the red rose of the House of Lancaster and the white rose of the House of York. The eventual reconciliation of the two houses gave rise to the Tudor rose, and Shakespeare immortalized that symbolism in his history plays.

The custom of giving flowers to express feelings gained momentum across Europe starting in the seventeenth century, when the so called Language of Flowers became popular after being brought over from Persia. Every flower came to carry a specific meaning, and the red rose secured its place as the number one messenger of romantic love.

In Brazil, Valentine's Day is celebrated on June 12, the eve of the feast of Saint Anthony, the patron saint of marriages and lonely hearts in Catholic tradition. That is no coincidence: the Brazilian date grew directly out of its closeness to the matchmaking saint.

Can cats actually be near roses?

Yes. According to the ASPCA, the leading reference in veterinary toxicology in North America, the rose (genus Rosa) is classified as non toxic to cats, dogs and horses. That includes the petals, leaves and stem.

The only real concern involves the thorns, which can cause small injuries to the mouth or paws of a curious cat. It is worth removing them before placing the arrangement somewhere the animal can reach. It is also good practice to rinse store bought roses, since they may have been treated with chemicals during cultivation.

Even though they are non toxic, no flower should really be part of a cat's diet. Ingesting large amounts of any plant material, even harmless material, can cause a temporary digestive upset simply because the feline digestive system was never built for it.

The real culprit may be hiding in the bouquet

Here is the detail few people know: baby's breath, that delicate little white flower used to add volume and lightness to bouquets, is not as harmless as it looks.

Baby's breath, genus Gypsophila, contains a compound called gyposenin, a saponin that can irritate a cat's gastrointestinal tract if ingested. Signs include vomiting, drooling, diarrhea, loss of appetite and lethargy. While generally not serious, the discomfort is real, and the plant's sweet scent tends to draw a cat's curiosity in exactly the wrong direction.

The good news is that there are equally pretty and safe alternatives to give a bouquet that extra touch. Statice and wax flower, for example, are considered safe for cats and do a fine job as visual filler without the same risk.

How to put together a cat friendly bouquet

In practice, securing a safe gift is simple. It just takes one sentence when placing the order:

"I'd like a bouquet of red roses, no baby's breath and no lilies please. I have a cat at home."

Lilies deserve special attention on that list because, unlike baby's breath, they are highly toxic to cats and can cause severe kidney failure even from minimal contact, such as pollen brushing onto fur during grooming.

With those two adjustments, roses remain exactly what they have always been: the most romantic gift in history, now also safe for whoever shares the couch with a demanding feline.
