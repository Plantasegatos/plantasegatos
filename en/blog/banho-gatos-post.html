Bathing Cats: Yes or No? Discover the Truth About Feline Hygiene

By Plantas & Gatos – Your trusted guide on feline care and behavior

🐱 Cats Are Masters of Cleanliness

If you live with a cat, you’ve probably noticed how much time they dedicate to grooming. Studies show that felines spend up to 5 hours a day licking themselves. Their tongues have tiny structures called papillae — little hooks that work like a natural brush, removing dust, loose hair, and spreading the skin’s natural oils.

💡 The result: cats are naturally clean and don’t need frequent baths to keep their coats healthy and beautiful.

🚫 Why Frequent Baths Can Be a Problem

Besides being unnecessary, too many baths can:

  • Strip away the skin’s natural protective oils;
  • Increase stress, since most cats dislike water;
  • Leave the coat dry and dull.

Cats value control of their environment. Being soaked, handled, and exposed to running water can be extremely uncomfortable for them.

✅ When a Bath Is Truly Necessary

Although rare, there are situations where a bath is recommended:

  • When your cat gets dirty with hazardous substances (paint, oil, toxic products);
  • On veterinarian’s advice, especially for skin issues;
  • For senior or sick cats who can no longer groom themselves properly;
  • Before specific medical procedures (such as surgeries).

In any of these cases, the bath should ideally be done by a trained professional who knows how to handle cats safely and use products made for them.

🪮 Brushing: The Best “Bath” for Your Cat

If you want to keep your cat’s coat clean and healthy, brushing is the secret! With just a few minutes a day, you can:

  • Remove dead hair and prevent mats;
  • Stimulate blood circulation;
  • Prevent hairballs (trichobezoars);
  • Strengthen the bond between you and your cat;
  • Keep the coat shiny.

Tip: choose a brush suited for your cat’s coat type and turn grooming into a moment of affection and relaxation.

In most cases, bathing is not necessary to keep cats clean. They are self-care experts, and daily brushing is enough to maintain a soft, shiny coat.

So, next time someone says “a cat that doesn’t get bathed is dirty,” you’ll know: that’s a myth! 😉
